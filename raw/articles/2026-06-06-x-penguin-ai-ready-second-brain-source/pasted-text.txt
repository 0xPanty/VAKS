How To Build An AI Ready Second Brain In Obsidian
most people use Obsidian like a prettier notes app, they save:
random ideas
useful threads
articles
videos
prompts
project notes
half-finished thoughts
then forget 80% of it exists, and the problem isn't Obsidian
the problem is that their second brain is only storage
a real second brain should help you:
remember, connect, decide, and reuse your own thinking
and AI makes this way more important
because Codex become much better when they can see your actual context
not just one prompt, but your patterns
1. Why most second brains fail
most second brains fail because people treat them like libraries
they focus on:
perfect folders
tags
dashboards
templates
graph view
aesthetic systems
but none of that matters if the notes do not help you think better
a second brain isn't useful because it stores more information
it becomes useful when it can answer questions like:
what do I keep thinking about?
what mistakes do I repeat?
what ideas are connected?
what do I already know about this topic?
what should I do next?
what did I try before?
that's the difference between a notes app and a thinking system
2. What your second brain should actually store
do not only save information, save FULL context
your vault should include:
ideas
observations
decisions
mistakes
projects
prompts
workflows
articles you like
posts you like
personal rules
things that feel fake
useful examples
repeated patterns

the goal is not to collect everything, your goal is to build a system that understands how you think
because AI is not only improved by more information, it's improved by better context
3. Simple folder structure
you don't need a complicated folder tree, start simple
example:
00 Inbox
01 Ideas
02 Projects
03 Research
04 Workflows
05 Prompts
06 Mistakes
07 Decisions
08 People / Companies
09 Writing / Voice
10 Archive
what each folder does:
00 Inbox
quick dump for anything you do not want to lose

01 Ideas
raw thoughts, article ideas, business ideas, random observations

02 Projects
things you are building, testing, writing, or planning

03 Research
articles, videos, tools, examples, market research

04 Workflows
repeatable systems you use more than once

05 Prompts
useful prompts for Claude, Codex, ChatGPT, NotebookLM, etc.

06 Mistakes
things that went wrong and what to avoid next time

07 Decisions
why you chose something, what tradeoff you accepted

08 People / Companies
people, creators, startups, companies, clients, competitors

09 Writing / Voice
your style, hooks, phrases, posts you like, things that feel fake

10 Archive
old notes that are not active but may still be useful later
you don't need to build a museum, just build a memory system
4. Every note needs a reason
this is the part most people skip
a note should not only say what happened
it should say why it matters
use this simple format:
What is this?
Why does it matter?
Where can I use it?
What does it connect to?
What question does it create?
example:
Topic: AI agents

What is this?
AI systems that can move tasks forward, not just answer questions.

Why does it matter?
Because boring repeated workflows are where agents become useful first.

Where can I use it?
Content research, lead research, code review, file organization, weekly reports.

What does it connect to?
[[AI workflows]]
[[Boring work]]
[[Automation]]
[[Claude]]
[[Codex]]

Question:
What tasks do I repeat every week that an agent could handle?
this makes the note useful later, not just saved
5. Why AI changes the second brain
before AI your second brain was mostly for you
now it can become context for AI
that changes everything
because AI is usually weak when it has:
no memory
no examples
no taste
no constraints
no history
no idea what you care about
that is why generic AI output feels empty, it doesn't know your patterns, it doesn't know your taste, it doesn't know what you tried before, it doesn't know what sounds fake to you
but when AI can read your vault, it can help with:
finding repeated patterns
summarizing what you already think
connecting old ideas
turning notes into plans
finding contradictions
improving decisions
creating better prompts
continuing projects without starting from zero
this is where Obsidian becomes way more powerful, not because it stores notes, but because it gives AI memory
6. What to ask Claude
once your vault has enough useful notes, you can start asking better questions
not random prompts, context prompts
use prompts like this:
Read my vault and find the 10 ideas I keep repeating.

Explain what they reveal about my thinking.

---

Find contradictions in my notes.

Where do I say one thing but act like another thing?

---

Look at my project notes.

Tell me what I should do next based on what I already wrote.

---

Take this messy note and turn it into:

- clear idea
- action steps
- related notes
- questions to answer next

---

Find everything in my vault related to [topic].

Summarize what I already know.

Then tell me what is missing.
this is the real value
AI becomes better when it can see the system behind your thinking
7. The weekly workflow
you do not need to live inside Obsidian all day
just run a simple loop
once a week:
1. dump new ideas into Inbox
2. move useful notes into folders
3. link notes that are obviously related
4. ask AI to find repeated patterns
5. turn the best patterns into projects / decisions / prompts
6. delete or archive useless noise
that's enough, the point isn't to organize perfectly, the point is to reduce friction, if your vault makes it easier to think, it is working
if it only makes you feel organized, it is probably just decoration
8. What makes this better than random AI chats
random AI chats reset too much
every new chat usually needs the same context again:
who you are
what you are building
what style you like
what you hate
what you already tried
what your goals are
what constraints matter
that's context waste, a second brain fixes this
instead of explaining yourself every time, you build a memory layer
AI stops being only a chatbot, it becomes something closer to an assistant that can understand your history
not perfectly, but enough to make the work feel less random
9. What this can become over time
after a while, your Obsidian vault can show you things you would normally miss
like:
topics you keep returning to
ideas you almost built but forgot
mistakes that repeat
workflows that should be automated
questions you still have not answered
projects that connect to each other
content ideas hidden inside old notes
business ideas hidden inside research
personal rules you keep learning the hard way
this is why the second brain idea actually matters
not because notes are cool, but because memory is leverage
the more useful context you store, the easier it becomes to think, build, write, decide, and use AI properly
